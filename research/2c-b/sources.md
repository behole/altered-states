## 1. Caudevilla-Gálligo et al. (2012) — Spanish 2C-B User Study

**Title:** 4-Bromo-2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine (2C-B): presence in the recreational drug market in Spain, pattern of use and subjective effects
**Authors:** Fermín Caudevilla-Gálligo, José Riba, Mónica Ventura, Daniel González, Magí Farré, María José Barbanoj, José Carlos Bouso
**Journal:** *Journal of Psychopharmacology*, 26(7), 1026–1035
**Year:** 2012
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22234927/ (DOI: 10.1177/0269881111431752)
**Tags:** [phenomenology] [human-survey] [qualitative]

**Annotation:** The largest structured human dataset on 2C-B's subjective profile:
recreational-market sampling in Spain with user-reported patterns of use and
effects questionnaires (N≈444 respondents). Documents the surreal yet
generally positive character, the social/small-gathering context of use, strong
visual effects, empathogenic qualities, and the notable absence of the
extreme darker sides reported for other new psychoactive drugs of that period.
Provides the skill's anchor for the "hybrid at normal intensities" claim: the
same reports describe *both* warm/social-emotional shifts and strong
kaleidoscopic visuals. Skill voice (talker's plateau, "sociable peak," music
sensitivity) is built primarily on this dataset.

## 2. Páleníček et al. (2013) — Rat Behavioral/Neurochemical/EEG Profile

**Title:** Behavioral, neurochemical and pharmaco-EEG profiles of the psychedelic drug 4-bromo-2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine (2C-B) in rats
**Authors:** Tomáš Páleníček, Michaela Fujáková, Martin Brunovský, Jiří Horáček, Ingmar Gorman, Marie Balíková, Lukáš Rambousek, Kamila Syslová, Petr Kačer, Petr Zach, Věra Bubeníková-Valešová, Filip Tylš, Anna Kubešová, Jana Puskarčíková, Cyril Höschl
**Journal:** *Psychopharmacology*, 225(1), 75–93
**Year:** 2013 (online 2012)
**URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-012-2797-7
**Tags:** [neuroscience] [preclinical] [dose-response]

**Annotation:** The best mechanistic behavioral characterization of 2C-B.
Biphasic locomotor profile (initial inhibition then excitation), PPI deficits,
NAc dopamine increase with decreased DOPAC — position 2C-B as a mixed
hallucinogen/entactogen/stimulant rather than cleanly any one of them. Supports
the skill's claim that the empathogenic and psychedelic channels co-occur: the
pharmacological signature is genuinely mixed-mode, not sequentially timed.
The dose-dependent EEG coherency shifts also informed the low/medium/high
intensity ladder.

## 3. Glennon, Titeler & Lyon (1988) — Receptor Binding

**Title:** A preliminary investigation of the psychoactive agent 4-bromo-2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine: a potential drug of abuse
**Authors:** Richard A. Glennon, Malgorzata Titeler, Raymond Lyon
**Journal:** *Pharmacology, Biochemistry, and Behavior*, 30(3), 597–601
**Year:** 1988
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3211969/
**Tags:** [neuroscience] [binding] [early]

**Annotation:** Early, foundational receptor work: 2C-B binds with high affinity
at 5-HT2 receptor sites — placing it mechanistically among the
serotonergic-hallucinogen family despite the 2C-series phenethylamine frame.
Used to establish the visual-dominance claim versus MDMA (which is not a
5-HT2A-agonist hallucinogen in this sense). Anchors the skill's "phenethylamine
but *not* simply LSD" positioning for the distinctive watercolor visual palette.

## 4. Yanakieva, Luke, Jansari & Terhune (2019) — Acquired Synesthesia Case Report

**Title:** Acquired synaesthesia following 2C-B use
**Authors:** Stoyka Yanakieva, Devin P. Luke, Aikaterini Jansari, Devin B. Terhune
**Journal:** *Psychopharmacology (Berl)*, 236(7), 2287–2289 (brief report)
**Year:** 2019
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31025060/ (DOI: 10.1007/s00213-019-05242-y)
**Tags:** [phenomenology] [case-report] [synesthesia]

**Annotation:** A peer-reviewed clinical case documenting *acquired,
persisting* synesthesia after a single 2C-B experience — laboratory-verified
and followed longitudinally. Directly grounds the skill's rule 10 (music =
visible motion) and the synesthesia dimension of the 7-dimension strip.
Clinically confirms that 2C-B's sensory-binding effects are salient enough to
be individually measurable against baseline — an unusually strong evidence
hook for a phenomenological simulation skill's synesthetic voice.

## 5. Shulgin & Shulgin (1991) — PIHKAL: Qualitative First-Person Catalog

**Title:** PIHKAL: A Chemical Love Story
**Authors:** Alexander Shulgin, Ann Shulgin
**Publisher:** Transform Press, Berkeley, CA
**Year:** 1991
**URL:** (book; standard library catalog entry)
**Tags:** [qualitative] [first-person] [primary-catalog]

**Annotation:** The original qualitative catalog for 2C-B's spectrum (Shulgin
synthesized it; the book's essay and experience-report sections carry the
"the world is beautiful and I feel love for it" classic voice — the origin of
2C-B's romantic/Valentine's reputation). **Limitation note:** PIHKAL-era
quantities are self-reported, informal, and unverified — this project cites
PIHKAL *only* for qualitative-voice evidence, never as a pharmacological or
behavioral authority. Any quantity language in this skill's intensity profiles
is framed as cited/known Shulgin catalog labels (museum / standard / upper
documented range), clearly attributed as informal historical descriptions, not
data.

## 6. de Boer et al. (1999) — Analytical Confirmation / Early Human Case Data

**Title:** More data about the new psychoactive drug 2C-B
**Authors:** D. de Boer, M. J. Gijzels, I. J. Bosman, R. A. A. Maes
**Journal:** *Journal of Analytical Toxicology*, 23(4), 227–228
**Year:** 1999
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10369336/
**Tags:** [analytical] [human-case] [context]

**Annotation:** Short analytical toxicology communication consolidating
successful identification of 2C-B in human biological samples against the
"confusable" substance classes (including the notorious bromo-substituted
compounds about to hit the same market). Grounds the *framing* layer of the
skill: 2C-B is a chemically identifiable, well-annealed compound whose
user-facing murky unknowns are lower than the grey-market average — one of the
documented reasons it shows up in relatively "clean," low-terror experiences
in contrast-skill stories.

## 7. Giroud et al. (1998) — Analytical Identification of 2C-B in Swiss Ecstasy Tablets

**Title:** 2C-B: A new psychoactive phenylethylamine recently discovered in
Ecstasy tablets sold on the Swiss black market
**Authors:** C. Giroud, M. Augsburger, L. Rivier, P. Mangin, F. Sadeghipour,
E. Varesio, J.-L. Veuthey, P. Kamalaprija
**Journal:** *Journal of Analytical Toxicology*, 22(5), 345–354
**Year:** 1998
**DOI:** 10.1093/jat/22.5.345 (PMID 9737327)
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9737327/
**Tags:** [analytical] [Swiss] [context]

**Annotation:** Forensic identification of 2C-B in two sets of tablets seized
on the Swiss black market, with HPLC-DAD/CE-DAD quantitation (3–8 mg per
tablet, in the range of the minimum effective dose). The Swiss primary record
of 2C-B's mid-1990s appearance on the European grey market, complementing
de Boer et al. (1999, Entry 6). Strictly analytical — it carries no
phenomenological or human-experience data, so the skill's
sensitivity/setting claims rest on Entries 1 and 5, not here.

**Provenance note:** this entry replaces a previously flagged citation,
"Heinzen et al. (1994)", an early-human Swiss report widely echoed in
secondary literature. Bibliographic searches (PubMed author query, Crossref,
Semantic Scholar, secondary reviews including Cole/Lea/Oxley 2002 and the
"2C or Not 2C" survey) found no such publication; it is treated as a
phantom/misattributed citation and was dropped. No claim in the dossier or
skill ever depended on it.
