# Tolerance & Repeat-Exposure — Annotated Source List

> **Project:** Altered States Skill Project
> **Last updated:** 2026-10-31
> **Sources:** 6 (5 peer-reviewed, 1 comprehensive review)
> **Scope:** Repeat-exposure phenomenology for the `skills/tolerance/` skill line:
> serotonergic day-two attenuation and complete cross-tolerance (psilocybin/LSD),
> and the heavy-pattern attenuated state described for ketamine. All sources are
> verified citations; none are constructed.

---

## 1. Isbell et al. (1961) — The Cross-Tolerance Landmark (Human)

**Title:** Cross tolerance between LSD and psilocybin
**Authors:** Harris Isbell, Alfred B. Wolbach, Abraham Wikler, E. James Minzer
**Journal:** *Psychopharmacologia*, 2(2), 147–159
**Year:** 1961
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00407974 (PubMed: 13717955)
**Tags:** [clinical] [tolerance] [human]

**Annotation:** The direct human evidence for complete cross-tolerance between LSD
and psilocybin. Two crossover-design experiments showed that volunteers exposed to
one substance responded to the other with a sharply reduced phenomenological
effect, and that direct tolerance to LSD developed across closely spaced uses of
LSD itself. This is the anchor citation for the entire "day-two" overlay line: it
establishes that the classical psychedelics share a single responsivity pool, so
re-encountering either substance reads like re-encountering the whole class.
Used by the LSD and psilocybin overlay skills to ground the shared, flattened
phenomenology. Verified: DOI resolvable, PubMed record exists.

## 2. de la Fuente Revenga, Jaster, McGinn, Silva, Saha & González-Maeso (2022) — Mechanism in Mice

**Title:** Tolerance and Cross-Tolerance among Psychedelic and Nonpsychedelic 5-HT2A
Receptor Agonists in Mice
**Authors:** Mario de la Fuente Revenga, Alaina M. Jaster, John McGinn, Gabriella
Silva, Somdatta Saha, Javier González-Maeso
**Journal:** *ACS Chemical Neuroscience*, 13(16), 2436–2448
**Year:** 2022
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acschemneuro.2c00170 (PMC: 10411500)
**Tags:** [neuroscience] [tolerance] [preclinical]

**Annotation:** Modern replication and mechanism: repeated psychedelic exposure
within a short window produced a progressive loss of the psychedelic behavioral
response, cross-tolerance between phenethylamine and ergoline psychedelics did NOT
generalize to a non-psychedelic 5-HT2A agonist, and a single exposure was
sufficient to down-regulate 5-HT2A receptor density in frontal cortex. Explains
*why* the day-two state is a shared-class state (the receptor pool is common) and
why the attenuation is perceptual-dominant rather than mood-selective. The overlay
skills draw the "same surface, lower gain" phenomenology from the behavioral
half-truth of this finding, translated qualitatively (explicitly flagged as
animal-model inference in the skills). Verified: DOI and PMC record exist.

## 3. Raval, Johansen, Donovan, Ros, Ozenne, Hansen & Knudsen (2021) — Single-Exposure Receptor Density

**Title:** A Single Dose of Psilocybin Increases Synaptic Density and Decreases
5-HT2A Receptor Density in the Pig Brain
**Authors:** Naja R. Raval, Anna Johansen, Louise L. Donovan, Nicolai F. Ros, Brice
Ozenne, Hans D. Hansen, Gitte M. Knudsen
**Journal:** *International Journal of Molecular Sciences*, 22(2), 835
**Year:** 2021
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms22020835
**Tags:** [neuroscience] [tolerance] [preclinical]

**Annotation:** PET-ligand imaging immediately after a single psilocybin exposure
showed reduced 5-HT2A receptor availability alongside increased synaptic density.
This is the cleanest single-exposure demonstration that the responsivity substrate
changes on the scale of the immediate re-encounter window, not only after heavy
repetition. Grounds the psilocybin day-two overlay's claim that the *next*
experience is of the same kind but at lower gain — and the claim that what does
persist (warmth, shifted valence, softer frame) is not simply "less of everything."
Verified: DOI resolvable.

## 4. Gresch, Smith, Barrett & Sanders-Bush (2005) — Behavioral Tolerance ↔ Receptor Signaling

**Title:** Behavioral tolerance to lysergic acid diethylamide is associated with
reduced serotonin-2A receptor signaling in rat cortex
**Authors:** Peter J. Gresch, Robert L. Smith, R. J. Barrett, Elaine Sanders-Bush
**Journal:** *Neuropsychopharmacology*, 30(9), 1693–1702
**Year:** 2005
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.npp.1300711
**Tags:** [neuroscience] [tolerance] [preclinical]

**Annotation:** Explicitly behavioral: tolerance to LSD's subjective proxy behaviors
developed in parallel with reduced cortical 5-HT2A signaling capacity. Ties the
human phenomenology of Isbell (1961) to the receptor-level mechanism the overlay
skills narrate qualitatively. Used for the LSD overlay's attribution of tolerance
to serotonergic responsivity — not to expectation, fatigue, or set-and-setting
drift. Verified: DOI resolvable.

## 5. Nichols (2016) — Comprehensive Review: Tolerance as Class Property

**Title:** Psychedelics
**Authors:** David E. Nichols
**Journal:** *Pharmacological Reviews*, 68(2), 264–355
**Year:** 2016
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1124/pr.115.011478
**Tags:** [pharmacology] [review] [tolerance]

**Annotation:** The definitive pharmacology review of the classical psychedelics.
Documents rapid development of (complete) tolerance to all subjective and
physiological effects of LSD-style agonists with repeated exposure, and states
tolerance/cross-tolerance across mescaline, psilocybin, and LSD — i.e., tolerance
is a property of the shared serotonergic mechanism, not of any one substance. The
tolerance skill line leans on this review to justify treating the psilocybin/LSD
day-two phenomenology as ONE named profile family with two per-substance overlays,
rather than two unrelated states. Verified: DOI resolvable; PMC available.

## 6. Morgan & Curran (2012) — Ketamine Chronic-Use Attenuation

**Title:** Ketamine use: a review
**Authors:** Celia J. A. Morgan, H. Valerie Curran
**Journal:** *Addiction*, 107(1), 27–38
**Year:** 2012
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1360-0443.2011.03576.x
**Tags:** [clinical] [tolerance] [ketamine]

**Annotation:** Review of the ketamine literature spanning recreational and
clinical exposure. Documents that with repeated heavy use, the dissociative and
hallucinatory effects attenuate while a distinctive dulling of cognition and
memory dominates instead — alongside dependence risk and dose escalation patterns.
This grounds the ketamine overlay's "burnout" phenomenology: the inner space
shrinks rather than deepens, the body and weather weaken, and the pattern-wise
thinking flattens into a slow, literal, exhausted fog. Critically, it also grounds
the overlay's cautions: burned-out ketamine phenomenology in fiction is written as
a degraded state, never as a target state. Verified: DOI resolvable; full text
available from multiple archives.

## Cross-cutting notes for the tolerance skill line

- **Complete mechanism statements (e.g., "5-HT2A down-regulates") are drawn from
  the preclinical sources (#2, #3, #4); the skills present them as research
  background, not as claims about any particular human experience.** Human
  evidence for the phenomenological shape is #1 (direct, whole-condition
  attenuation) and #5 (review). Ketamine human phenomenology of attenuation is #6.
- **No source above licenses any usage guidance.** The tolerance skills describe
  how an already-loaded state changes on re-encounter — they contain no spacing,
  persistence, reset, or recovery recommendations of any kind, per `SAFETY.md`
  and the project-wide content contract: no "wait N days" phrasing, no
  day-numbered state instructions, no reset percentages stated as usage guidance.
